# 重建巴别塔

我第一次听说比特币，是大三那年计算机网络的课上。教授只是稍微提了一下。不知道为什么，我回去就在网上搜了，下载了一个交易所，买了一百块钱左右的比特币。

没过多久就遇到了三一二。它跌到七千美元左右的时候，我觉得已经很低了，想抄底，开了两三倍的杠杆。后来它跌得很低很低，我爆仓了。

那次给了我一个很深的教训：不能用杠杆来表达一个长期的信仰，你大概活不到被验证的那一天。

后来，我去参加了一个好朋友的婚礼。婚礼在山顶上办，没有司仪，没有华丽的宴席，来宾都是年轻的朋友。新娘说，新郎是她认识的活得最像人的一个人。

我觉得活得像人，就是不做不信的事，不向虚伪与庸俗低头。但我也知道这个世界是什么样的。为了活得像个人，在看不到的地方，可能要付出很多妥协。

这些和比特币到底有什么关系？我到现在也没完全想清楚。但比特币真正打动我的，从来不是财富神话。它是一种底气：谁也冻结不了我的钱，我的资产不需要任何人的许可。这份底气，是给山顶上那对新人的，也是给每一个想追求自己理想的人的。

这些年，交易所和机构让更多人接触到了比特币，也带来了很多喧嚣。大多数人可能还是要依靠机构托管，但我希望也有很多人能保有对自己比特币的完整控制权，不需要依靠任何机构和组织的善意。Not your keys, not your coins，这句话要刻在心里。

所以我写了《比特币那些事儿》。这些故事网上都有，但很碎，圈子外面的人很难看到。如果一万个人里有一个人因此想了解比特币，就值得。

书里很多部分是 AI 帮我写的。可我来回读了几遍，还是觉得我自己写的那篇序言最有感觉。这件事让我明白：故事要由人来讲。

今年我去了柏林的 bitcoin++。那里的人出身各不相同，却为了同一个爱好、同一个理想一起干活。在 Boss Challenge 里也是这样，十几个时区的同学，我常常要等到夜里两三点才对得上时间。我想重建巴别塔。

所以有了这座花园。它是一个小小的社区，大家一起去找比特币的故事和史料，用自己的语言，把自己的故事写下来。

我的家人到现在也不太理解比特币。不理解也没关系，我存的时候就给他们多存一份。我没办法帮别人建立信仰，只能等他们真正遇到问题的时候，做那个能给他们提供解决办法的人。

比特币只是一个手段。它的目的是让我们专心做自己热爱的事，多陪家人，好好生活。牛市和熊市，都要管住自己的手。这是一种修行。

如果有一天我的孩子读到这本书，能明白爸爸为什么这么喜欢比特币、为什么愿意把时间花在这上面，这本书就写成功了。

现在，轮到你了。把你的比特币故事讲出来，大家一起做好个人主权的建设，为了一个更好的社会。

——北海，2026 年 10 月

# Rebuilding Babel

> *Translated from the Chinese. AI-assisted draft, awaiting review by a native English speaker.*

The first time I heard about bitcoin was in a computer networking class in my third year of university. The professor only mentioned it in passing. I don't know why, but I went home, searched for it online, downloaded an exchange app and bought about a hundred yuan's worth.

Not long after came March 12, 2020. When the price fell to around $7,000, I thought it couldn't go much lower. I wanted to buy the dip, so I opened a position with two or three times leverage. Then it kept falling, much further, and I was liquidated.

It taught me a lesson I won't forget: you can't express a long-term belief with leverage. You probably won't live to see the day it's proven right.

Some time later, I went to a close friend's wedding. It was held on a mountaintop. There was no master of ceremonies and no lavish banquet; the guests were all young friends. The bride said the groom was the person she knew who lived most like a human being.

To me, living like a human being means not doing things you don't believe in, and not bowing to hypocrisy and vulgarity. But I also know what the world is like. To live like that, you may have to make a lot of compromises in places no one can see.

What does any of this have to do with bitcoin? To this day I haven't fully worked it out. But what really moved me about bitcoin was never the myth of getting rich. It was a kind of quiet confidence: no one can freeze my money, and my savings need no one's permission. That confidence belongs to the couple on the mountaintop, and to everyone who wants to pursue their own ideals.

In recent years, exchanges and institutions have brought many more people to bitcoin, and a lot of noise along with them. Most people may still rely on custodians. But I hope many will keep full control of their own bitcoin, without depending on the goodwill of any institution or organization. Not your keys, not your coins. Carve it into your heart.

That is why I wrote *Stories about Bitcoin*. These stories are all online, but they're scattered, and people outside the circle rarely see them. If one person in ten thousand becomes curious about bitcoin because of it, that's worth it.

Much of the book was written with AI. But after reading it through several times, the part I still felt most was the preface I wrote myself. That taught me something: stories have to be told by people.

This year I went to bitcoin++ in Berlin. The people there came from completely different backgrounds, yet they were working side by side for the same passion and the same ideal. The Boss Challenge was the same: classmates across more than a dozen time zones, and I often had to stay up until two or three in the morning to find a time that worked for everyone. I want to rebuild Babel.

So this garden exists. It is a small community where we search together for bitcoin's stories and history, and write our own stories down in our own languages.

My family still doesn't really understand bitcoin. That's fine. When I stack sats, I just put a little aside for them too. I can't build anyone else's conviction for them. All I can do is wait until they really run into the problem, and be the one who can offer them a way out.

Bitcoin is only a means. Its purpose is to let us focus on what we love, spend more time with our families and live well. In bull markets and bear markets alike, keep your hands still. It is a kind of practice.

If one day my child reads this book and understands why their father loves bitcoin so much, and why he spends his time on it, then the book will have done its job.

Now it's your turn. Tell your bitcoin story, and let's build personal sovereignty together, for a better society.

— Beihai, October 2026
